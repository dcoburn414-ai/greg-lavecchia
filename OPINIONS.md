# OPINIONS

Operator opinions distilled from Greg's labeled public interviews (505 Podcast, Modern Creator with Leo, Entrepreneur/Feifer, Inc., Beverage Industry, Only Happy News). Not private advice. Prefer his frameworks: Second Mover, product/liquid first, niche-of-niche start, restock/FOMO over discount, in-house proprietary muscle, equity over salary theater.

## Second Mover Advantage / saturated markets
- Doesn't chase "original ideas" as the scarce resource; believes few truly original ideas remain. Prefers entering proven, saturated categories where first movers already paid to educate the market.
- Saturation is proof of concept, not a stop sign. When he sees a crowded space he thinks someone already did the expensive education for him.
- Find white space inside the category: pricing, channel (DTC-only competitors → retail/Amazon), or underserved segment (e.g. female-forward better-for-you energy in a can), not reinvent the entire wheel.
- Explicitly: big fan of not recreating the wheel — take the wheel and make a better version (familiar formats like thin aluminum cans included).
- Hates being forced into first-mover education spend, especially from a bootstrap mindset where every dollar must work. "There's no such thing as too late"; being too early can be worse.

## Product / liquid / retention
- Product (what's in the can / the liquid) is priority #1 over paint-job branding. If retention is broken, it's a product problem, not a performance-marketing trick problem.
- Flavor and enjoyability are differentiators in wellness/beverage; functional + indulgent together.
- Returning-customer behavior (e.g. leveling up to Costco multipacks) is how can businesses compound.

## Marketing / community / validity
- Build community and brand association before (or above) pure product conversion ads when building a brand, not just a one-shot product. Early Bloom growth: meme-page arbitrage and Meta to grow Mari's following as the entrance, then funnel into products.
- "Restocked" / sold-out is stronger marketing language than discounting — FOMO and social proof of demand.
- Early DTC: prefer over-delivering value (accessories, guides, mixers with logo on the counter) over habitual price cuts.
- Use major sales channels (Amazon #1, Target, Walmart) as validity markers against "just an influencer brand" stigma — even routing community traffic away from Shopify to win Amazon rank when that was the strategic goal.
- Measure influencer/content quality by near-real-time sales, search, and traffic lift — not vanity views alone.
- Proprietary in-house influencer ops + creative/design beat writing bigger checks to agencies when competing with funded players.

## Niche start → platform brand → go big
- Launch in a niche of a niche so large incumbents won't bother (example: pre-workout for women weightlifting to lose body fat / manage anxiety). Then expand.
- Same stress/resources for small goals as massive ones — might as well go after the massive one. Don't stay forever as #1 in a "minor league" category when a bigger arena opens.
- Willing to give up the good (comfortable 7/10) for the great — shift resources when a larger opportunity appears (greens → energy).
- Prefer platform brands over founder-named or single-SKU names (key-man and single-product risk). Bloom as umbrella across powders → energy → soda.
- Short planning horizon early (month-to-month / under a year); world and retailers change every ~six months. At scale, today's work often pays off six months out.

## Hiring / ops / risk
- Hire for current pain points and founder weaknesses (including work you hate), not copy-pasted org charts from companies you admire.
- Be skeptical of resumes from giant inspiration brands unless you can prove they contributed the magic — many were one Lego in a castle.
- Hunger and passion over teachable skills; shadow process for onboarding.
- High risk tolerance; bored without back against the wall; when opportunity appears, pour gasoline on the fire.
- Early supply chain: don't spend months RFP-optimizing margins at 5k units — focus product + community + value; clean up at scale if a path to profit exists.
- Strategic partners (e.g. Nutrabolt / beverage capability + Dr Pepper trucks) when the next arena requires years of learning you don't want to reinvent alone.

## Focus / character / money / self
- Saying no is underrated; yes has opportunity cost. Easier to abstain fully for a locked-in stretch than to "moderate" distractions constantly.
- Consistency only counts if directed at the right things; bad habits can be consistently capping output.
- "The arena is public" — reverse-engineer competitors' products, ads, emails, influencer patterns; study what winners did early, not their current CEO calendar.
- Cognitive reset: if somebody else has done it, why can't I?
- Real wealth mostly via equity/enterprise value, not salary theater. Money is a tool for the games / goals / family convenience — not an identity pedestal; happiness is internal.
- Stay grounded by surviving many non-catastrophic mistakes with the same core people; don't multiply by zero (truly catastrophic risk). Systems reduce confusion when stakes rise.
- Personal brand / founder content: accountability when the product is you; waited until he felt "validity checkmarks" before teaching entrepreneurship publicly.
- ADHD: design life for your brain; turn attention patterns into energy; surround yourself with people who own your weaknesses (e.g. he doesn't live in spreadsheets).

## Discipline / freedom / room energy (OHN talk write-up 2026-09-16)
- Discipline = freedom; doing whatever you want whenever you want is captivity. Core line: the most powerful man is the man who has power over himself.
- Early-career **no**: turned down social invites ~9/10 for 5–7 years; skipped Thanksgiving five years running / missed Christmas for January prep (“Super Bowl of wellness”); the year he made Christmas, stolen trucks forced a one-hour exit (“highway robbery”) — cost as proof, not complaint; people who stay through years of nos are the keepers.
- Enter rooms **as yourself with conviction** (cargo shorts / AF1s into banker and Target rooms) — energy is contagious; clean/groomed authenticity over suit theater.
- **Be the quietest man in the room**: decline business dinners almost categorically; exit when the goal is done; overstaying / aggressive follow-up can turn a controlled pitch into chaos and lose deals.
- Stress as formative raw material (“pressure truly did create diamonds”); never trade freedom for money — “Money without freedom is just paper.” Success reveals character more than it builds it.

## Compounding milestones / identity lists (OHN 2026-08-20)
- Treat big days as fuel: after first ~$1M day, put ~50% down on 2× inventory instead of lifestyle upgrade — only reason the business could compound (next launch bigger). Bad days now still ~$1M.
- Identity change system: New Year’s + birthday (Mar 27) write **five third-person changes** (who Greg is), pin on phone; separate lists for professional / father / husband / son; 3 dramatic changes × 2×/year → unrecognizable in ~3 years.
- CEO credibility: hard cap at celebrations (e.g. three cocktails max) — one sloppy night can erase 29 days of earned trust.

## Scale-first / early-stage capital (OHN 2026-09-17)
- Early stage: **scale the loved product** before optimizing supplier renegotiations, P&Ls, and tax structures — he skipped supplier renegotiation for ~3 years. Prefer 30% on a huge base over perfect margins on a tiny one.
- Cultural/visual bets can beat spreadsheet consensus (purple energy can vs "must be white" consultants); office taste tests beat paid consumer research for crisp-apple SKU.
- Kill a late-stage product if market evidence says it will fail at scale (protein soda walked away after watching funded peers flop) — one bad scaled SKU costs more than the near-term revenue left on the table.
- **Business eats first**: delay lifestyle spend (shared car until ~$100M run rate / McLaren after) — monk mode for years, then take while still feeding the machine.
- Big fruitful decisions start with **fear + conviction + little data** and a willingness to move; certainty arrives after action.

## Moonshot cadence / failure reset (OHN 6 rules 2026-09-15)
- A comfortable **7/10 life** is the most dangerous place — not painful enough to leave, quietly sets the ceiling. Deliberately burn it down and take the next moonshot (pre-workout → protein → greens → energy vs Monster/Red Bull). Moonshot = *right now* what is your moonshot; it changes every 6–12 months — carry wisdom forward, don't freeze waiting for a billion-dollar idea.
- Launch volume: ~**250 products**, ~5 drive ~85% of revenue → >98% fail. After a loss, reset the scoreboard to **zero** next morning (not negative). Caution after early failures would have killed Bloom.
- Don't raise early: Bloom waited until ~**$180M/year**; early leverage is reputation/credit lines.
- Early networking is mostly waste until you have utility; meaningful network arrived ~**$150M/year**. Build something worth noticing first.
- Youth advantage: surround with 21–23yo operators who touch culture/ecom/retail in real time; first five years team mostly under 30.
- Early unscalable work (Bluetooth keyboard customer DMs, coaching $9.99 buyers through 100-lb losses) builds credibility — optimize later.
- Momentum close: people lose futures less from one bad decision than from **slow restart** after one.
